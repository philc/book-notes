Book Notes

The Obstacle Is the Way - Ryan Holiday

Gems

  • Stop over-thinking the future. It comes at the cost of the present. We should enjoy our current moments and be effective in them.
  • On seeking "the process," rather than the championship
    • The process: existing in the present, excelling at each step, focused on what you can do and can control.
    • "The process is relaxing." With this framing, the hardest things become manageable.
      • "Break it down into pieces. Simply do what you need to do right now. And do it well. And then move on to the next thing."
    • "The process is about finishing the small task in front of you."
  • Love everything that happens, don't just "bear with it." It's all fuel.
  • Using something bigger than ourselves for motivation
    • Pivoting one's perspective away from ourselves, towards others, is galvanizing: it immediately provides something to do. A calling.
  • "In the shadow of death, prioritization is easier. As are graciousness and appreciation and principles."

Intro

  • We're taught paths to success, but not how to overcome failure. Some can use obstacles as launch pads to success.
  • "The things which hurt, instruct." - Benjamin Franklin.

The discipline of perception (chap 1)

  • Discard emotion, and what's left is truth / reality.
  • Be cool and objective.
  • Example: Rockerfeller made gains where others were fearful or greedy.
    • "The market was inherently unpredictable and often vicious — only the rational and disciplined mind could hope to profit from it. Speculation led to disaster, he realized, and he needed to always ignore the 'mad crowd' and its inclinations."
    • "He was inclined to see the opportunity in every disaster."
  • "Everything that happens — be it an economic crash or personal tragedy — is a chance to move forward."

Recognize your power (chap 2)

  • How we perceive situations is always our choice, and cannot be taken from us.
  • Situations are neither good nor bad. Only our attitudes make them so.
  • On employee mistakes: "With a shift in perception, it can be exactly what you were looking for — the chance to pierce through defenses and teach a lesson that can be learned only by experience. A mistake becomes training."

Control your emotions (chap 4)

  • Uncertainty and fear are relieved by authority. Training is authority. It's a release valve.
    • Astronauts are trained to rely on their training, and keep emotions like fear and panic from affecting their actions.
    • The problems and stakes of space travel make our terrestrial high-stress situations, like speaking in front of a crowd, look inconsequential.

Is it up to you? (chap 7)

  • "Focusing exclusively on what is in our power magnifies and enhances our power."
    • Struggling against the unchanging rules of the game or lamenting past events is wasted effort.

Live in the present moment (chap 8)

  • "In our own lives, we aren't content to deal with things as they happen. We have to dive endlessly into what everything 'means', whether something is 'fair' or not, what's 'behind' this or that, and what everyone else is doing. Then we wonder why we don't have the energy to actually deal with our problems."
  • Stop over-thinking the future. It comes at the cost of the present. We should enjoy our current moments and be effective in them.

Finding the opportunity (chap 10)

  • "The struggle against an obstacle inevitably propels the fighter to a new level of functioning. The extent of the struggle determines the extent of the growth. The obstacle is an advantage, not adversity."
    • (A common example is athletes who recover from and overcome a serious injury.)

Practice persistence (chap 14)

  • Work on the obstacle from every angle, repeatedly. Persistence is a good substitute for genius.

Follow the process (chap 16)

  • The process: existing in the present, excelling at each step, focused on what you can do and can control.
  • "The process is relaxing." In this way, the hardest things become manageable.
    • "Break it down into pieces. Simply do what you need to do right now. And do it well. And then move on to the next thing."
  • The nature of problems:
    • Every hard road is a system of connected composite parts that can be completed in sequence.
    • This knowledge will bring us comfort and confidence.
    • "We shy away from writing a book or making a film even though it's our dream because it's so much work — we can't imagine how we get from here to there."

Love everything that happens: amor fati (chap 29)

  • Love everything that happens, don't just "bear with it." It's all fuel.
  • "We've got to love what we do and all that it entails, good and bad. We have to learn to find joy in every single thing that happens."

Something bigger than yourself (chap 31)

  • Being the kind of person we believe others should be
    • "When we focus on others, on helping them or simply providing a good example, our own personal fears and troubles will diminish."
  • Pivoting one's perspective away from ourselves, towards others, is galvanizing: it immediately provides something to do. A calling.

Meditate on your mortality (chap 32)

  • "It's a story as old as time. Man nearly dies, he takes stock, and emerges from the experience a completely different, and better, person."
  • "Death doesn't make life pointless, but rather purposeful."
  • Why do we spend our time making more money than we could spend in a lifetime?
  • "They figure out what they need to do and do it, fitting in as much as possible before the clock expires. They figure out how, when the moment strikes, to say, 'Of course, I would have liked to last a little longer, but I made a lot out of what I was already given so this works too.'"
  • "In the shadow of death, prioritization is easier. As are graciousness and appreciation and principles."
  • "Death creates urgency, and urgency is invigorating."
